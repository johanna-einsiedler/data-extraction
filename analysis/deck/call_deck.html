Three things changed while preparing this. The tuning run is not a converged optimisation — TPE never engaged, so the 96 trials are an unbalanced randomised experiment, which is better for causal claims about design and worse for finding an optimum. A scoring fix we were about to apply would have destroyed 17 trials. And the single largest practical result has nothing to do with model choice.

Part 1

What the runs say

Seventeen trials were unusable, and we got them back. Trials 382–398 carried no model outputs: they were ingested from the OpenAI Batch API, and both ingest scripts score each answer and then discard it, writing only {query_id, type, doc_name, accuracy}. But the raw batch files were still on disk, and they hold the answers, the log-probabilities and the per-call token counts. Recovering them lifts the analysis set from 70 to 87 — every completed trial that covers all 35 papers — raises log-probability coverage by a third (45,314 → 60,359 cells), and triples the ground truth available for validating cost.

The scoring fix we were about to apply would have destroyed those 17. rescore_concat_fix.py re-scores from stored outputs, so with --write it would have marked every one of their answers wrong — in the raw dry run that looked like a legitimate 9–14 point accuracy correction. Applied only where it is valid, the fix is monotone: 2,351 cells corrected 0→1 and zero the other way. It now runs as a read-only sidecar, and the unsafe script has been retired.

The fix moves the argmax from trial #31 to #336, but leaves the ranking intact (Spearman ρ = 0.986, top-10 overlap 9/10). That distinction matters more than it sounds, because of the next figure.

There is no best configuration, only a top set. The gap between the top configs is smaller than the noise. Reporting a single winner would be reporting sampling noise, and the Phase 2 search should target a stable high-performing region instead.

This is the biggest practical result in the study. Batched full-document inference costs $0.022/paper against $0.661 for one call per item — 31× cheaper, on 25 and 21 trials respectively. It is 35 model calls per paper instead of 1,505, and it is not worse: batched is 0.008 above individual on median accuracy, a difference we cannot distinguish from zero. A full 198-paper test run costs about $4 instead of $131.

This number moved, and it is worth knowing why. On the pre-recovery sample the gap looked like 41× and we called it a conservative floor, because individual mode appeared to be under-estimated. That rested on two trials with recorded token counts. The recovered batch files supply twelve more, and against fourteen the estimator is essentially unbiased (median ratio 0.96). The honest figure is 31×, not 41× — and it is a point estimate, not a floor.

Two results overturn what the descriptive tables suggested. Reasoning hurts (−0.102, p < .001; −0.130 among full-document trials) — and unlike the raw marginal, this is adjusted for which model was used. And retrieval buys nothing: RAG versus full-document inference is −0.011 (p = .47), a null.

What we cannot estimate, and will not plot. The RAG branch has 36 trials against roughly 50 parameters. An unguarded fit returned coefficients of 1010 with standard errors of 1016 — it would have looked like a filled-in table. After pruning to levers with at least five trials per level, only chunker, embedder and query-embedding survive. Chunk size, chunk overlap, retriever, k, reranking and token budget have no support at all and are reported as "not identified".

The model is scored against a moving target. Under the same normalisation the pipeline is scored with, the two human coders disagree on 25.7% of cells — 51% on open-ended items. So ~79% against the adjudicated gold sits against a ~74% human ceiling, and the items the model fails are the items humans argue about (r = −0.26). Raw per-item accuracy will mislead readers unless we also report it normalised by the ceiling.

At 35 tuning papers we can only resolve about 4.1 points — larger than the spread among our best configurations. Detecting 2 points needs roughly 100 papers. Pairing two configurations on the same papers buys only ~16%, because papers are not uniformly easy; so racing and early stopping help by killing bad configurations quickly, not by resolving close calls.

Part 2

Nine decisions

In the order they were raised. Each ends with what we need to settle on the call.

1Cost and whether to cap it

Cost is now reconstructed for all 93 recoverable trials at zero API spend. Worth saying plainly: two-coder human extraction runs roughly $40–80 per paper, so even our most expensive configuration is 20–40× cheaper than people. A cap is a budget constraint, not a scientific one. Proposal: a hard eligibility cap of $0.50/paper, which excludes almost nothing currently competitive, and report accuracy against cost as a second dimension rather than optimising a blend. Batched mode should be promoted to a first-class arm.

Decision needed. Do we accept $0.50/paper as the eligibility cap, and what is the real remaining API budget?

2How the search should work

TPE never engaged — 96 completed trials against n_startup_trials=100 — so every trial came from the random prior. It also cannot exclude anything, only down-weight, and the objective is a mean over 35 noisy instances. Proposal: a preregistered three-stage screen → race → confirm. Screen levels on the 116k cells we already have (free); race configurations in a new study with WilcoxonPruner and a grouped multivariate sampler; confirm the top five on the full tuning set with a written tie-break ladder. Exclusion rule: drop a level only when the upper bound of its 95% interval is below −0.02, with a floor that no level with fewer than five trials is ever excluded on statistical evidence.

Decision needed. Accept “stable region, not argmax” as the goal, the exclusion rule, and ~$450–850 for the remaining trials?

3Reporting the design effects

Spec A above is the confirmatory answer to RQ1.2, with one honesty clause: the trials are an unbalanced sample with cost-correlated missingness, so these are adjusted associations over the region we sampled, not clean causal contrasts. The one-factor-at-a-time ladder around the frozen winner is what converts the highest-stakes comparisons into real experiments.

Decision needed. Bayesian hierarchical model (handles the sparse levels gracefully) or frequentist? We recommend Bayesian.

4Question type and rater disagreement

Both are now computable and the normalisation is settled. Two asks. First, report ceiling-normalised accuracy alongside raw, or items like 5.2 and 1.3 will read as model failures when they are codebook failures. Second, a structural problem: the diff_human and diff_llm ratings are per study × rater, not per item, so RQ1.7 is only answerable at study level as designed. A 20-minute item-level difficulty ranking from two to four coders would upgrade it to a genuine preregistered prediction — but it has to happen before the test set is scored.

Decision needed. Do we run the item-level difficulty elicitation, and accept ceiling-normalised accuracy as co-primary?

5The agentic arm

Not in the Phase 1 preregistration, so it would enter as exploratory-confirmatory: conditions and hypotheses fixed in advance, all six run on the tuning set only, and only the winner touches the test set. Six conditions isolate agency, the codebook, and examples — the interesting one being whether a model that writes its own codebook from ten labelled examples matches an agent given ours. Roughly $85–150 and a week of wall-clock. It is the cheapest genuinely novel thing on this list.

Decision needed. Approve the arm and its framing, with gpt-oss-120b fixed as the single driver model?

6Calibration

With 15 calibration papers we have 645 cells — enough for a marginal conformal guarantee, not enough per item, and the list type fails outright. The near-free fix: move 35 papers from test to calibration using the same seed-42 permutation, which gives 50 calibration papers and restores the test set to the preregistered 163 rather than shrinking it. Everything so far ran at temperature 0, so self-consistency needs new runs; it is also the only uncertainty signal available for the five models that expose no log-probabilities.

Decision needed. Approve the re-split (calibration 50 / test 163), and a learned combined score or plain log-probabilities?

7Conformal prediction and propagating uncertainty

For multiple-choice, do not conformalise the power set — conformalise each option separately, which maps onto the binary machinery we already have. Set the operating point at the smallest flag rate whose auto-accepted accuracy beats the two-coder agreement rate on those same cells, so the success criterion is “at least as good as a second human coder”. Downstream, prediction-powered inference over all 500 papers, with the flip indicator against the review's own thresholds. If a substantive conclusion flips across our top five configurations, that is the most important finding in the paper.

Decision needed. Which eight adherence estimands? Please bring the review's claim list to the call.

8Evaluation-set size

Reduce the search set, never the evaluation set — conflating the two is the trap. The test set carries RQ1.1 and every subgroup analysis, and it is the one place lost power costs a preregistered question. For screening, 20 papers and 25 items is about a third of the per-trial cost and still resolves the ~4.6 point differences that matter at that stage, with the full set restored at confirmation.

Decision needed. Approve 20 papers × 25 items for screening only, with the stated hard-item selection bias?

9The model roster

Adding a model invalidates the existing study — a new categorical level makes every recorded trial incompatible. But the search redesign forces a new study anyway, so the roster refresh is free. Re-run the entry exam against the live provider catalogues rather than any published list, freeze it to a dated snapshot, verify log-probability support empirically rather than trusting metadata, and cut from 17 models to about 12 so each level gets more trials. Then run a model-swap-only sweep at the frozen winning configuration — that paired curve is a far cleaner answer to RQ1.5 than regressing over unbalanced trials.

Decision needed. Fresh study, 12 models with at least 8 exposing log-probabilities, and the paired swap as the primary RQ1.5 analysis?
